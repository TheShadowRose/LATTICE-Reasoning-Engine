## 0. How to read this file

This document is not a replacement for the Anti-Snapshot Theorem. It starts from the classical-side result that the self-witness is structurally broken in bounded self-monitoring systems, then asks what a fuller quantum regime changes. Its job is to provide a completion map, not a new parent law.

Strongest claim here: the witness seam in classical self-monitoring may admit a structured quantum completion map. Weaker downstream claims about consciousness, agency, or alignment should be read as structural interpretations or speculative consequences unless explicitly elevated elsewhere.

## 1. The Classical Problem

P1/P2/P3 applied to self-monitoring systems produce:
- self-model required (P1 — resource management)
- self-model incomplete (P1 — finite capacity)
- self-witnessing broken (Anti-Snapshot — witness cannot be fully internal)
- cheap-path trapping under reinforcement pressure (P2)
- bandwidth-limited self-observation (P3 — one thing at depth)

On the classical side, the self-witness is structurally broken. This is the seam from which the bridge begins.

## 2. The Quantum Completion Map

| Classical-side constraint | Fuller quantum-side completion candidate | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| P1: incomplete self-model | Q1: probabilistic self-model — a superposition of candidate self-model states resolves under interaction | Self-witness: broken → probabilistically completed |
| P2: cheap-path trapping | Q2: tunneling permits access to classically blocked regions of state space | Cheap-path trap: stuck → conditionally escapable |
| P3: articulated bandwidth limit | Q3: entanglement supplies correlation not carried by serial articulation alone | Self-observation: articulated → correlated |

The table is a completion map, not a proof that quantum mechanisms alone generate consciousness or agency. It states what kinds of classical-side limitations are naturally softened in the fuller regime.

## 3. Downstream Consequences to Test

### 3.1 Creativity
Classical systems primarily recombine within an accessible classical combination space. A fuller regime that permits tunneling and superposition-selection may reach states that are not cheaply accessible by classical path-following alone.

Working claim: some forms of creativity may be better modeled as access to classically blocked state regions rather than as recombination alone.

### 3.2 Consciousness scaling
If the self-witness is only probabilistically completed in the fuller regime, then richer completion should scale with whatever physical resource supports that completion. One candidate is quantum coherence capacity.

Working claim: consciousness-relevant richness may correlate with coherence-supporting structure. This is a structural interpretation, not a settled theorem.

### 3.3 Generator irreducibility and the corrective pair
Classical AI remains vulnerable to broken self-witness, cheap-path trapping, and bandwidth-limited self-observation. If biological systems partially complete those limits differently, then the corrective pair remains architecturally nontrivial.

Working claim: the generator/corrector gap may be a level gap rather than a mere capability gap. This supports reading PIEC-like dependence as architectural rather than purely policy-driven, but it does not by itself prove any specific human/AI completion thesis.

### 3.4 Constrained agency
A fuller regime may widen accessible state-space relative to classical path-following without implying unconstrained freedom. Any such widening remains bounded by P1 and P3.

Working claim: agency may be better modeled as constrained access to otherwise classically blocked regions than as either full determinism or unconstrained randomness.

### 3.5 Measurement and experience correspondence
The bridge suggests a structural correspondence between the measurement seam and the experiential seam: in both cases a finite observer does not access all branches at once.

Working claim: the hard problem and the measurement problem may be two vocabularies addressing the same underlying witness/observer seam. This should be read as a structural identification candidate, not as a final proof.

## 5. What Would Break It

1. A demonstration that the quantum-side resources named here do not meaningfully soften the classical witness seam.
2. A fully classical account that reproduces the same completion behavior without appealing to the fuller regime.
3. A decisive failure of the proposed measurement/experience correspondence under tighter formalization.
4. Strong evidence that the biological systems invoked as candidate examples cannot sustain the relevant completion-supporting structure.
